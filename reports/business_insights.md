# Business Insights & Recommendations

## Scope and caveat

This report analyses 360 synthetic order records from 2025. It is a learning exercise, not an assessment of a real retailer. Profit is an illustrative estimate calculated using assumed category cost ratios. All recommendations below are hypotheses to test with real operational data.

## KPI snapshot

|---|---:|
| Orders | 360 |

| Average order value | ₹3,286.62 |

## Findings

### 1. Regional sales

South leads the four sample regions with ₹3,26,299.00 in sales, followed by North (₹3,11,441.45), West (₹2,94,349.05) and East (₹2,51,094.60). The gap between the top and bottom region is a useful prompt for deeper investigation, not proof of a real regional preference.

**Possible action to test:** compare real store coverage, product availability, order volume, promotion exposure and fulfilment performance by region before allocating additional budget.

### 2. Category mix

Electronics contributes the highest sample sales at ₹3,58,636.45, narrowly ahead of Home & Kitchen at ₹3,51,876.15. Fashion contributes ₹3,36,873.95, while Beauty contributes ₹1,35,797.55.

**Possible action to test:** review category-level conversion, stock-outs, returns and contribution margin. High sales alone do not guarantee the strongest commercial return.

### 3. Monthly trend

June 2025 is the strongest month in the sample at ₹1,21,253.65. Monthly patterns should be checked against promotions, seasonality, inventory and channel activity in any real dataset.

**Possible action to test:** compare year-over-year and prior-period performance, then annotate the trend with campaign and holiday dates.

### 4. Payment method

Card is the most frequently used payment method (100 orders), followed by Cash (91), UPI (88) and Net Banking (81).

**Possible action to test:** compare payment success rate, transaction fees, refunds and customer experience. Frequency alone is not enough to decide which payment option is most valuable.

## Recommended next analysis with real data

1. Add returns, cancellations and refunds to calculate net sales.
2. Replace assumed product costs with verified cost-of-goods data.
3. Add customer, channel, store and campaign dimensions where permitted.
4. Compare conversion, repeat purchase, stock-outs and fulfilment times.
5. Track trends across multiple years before making seasonal or forecasting claims.

## Conclusion

The project demonstrates a complete descriptive analytics workflow: validate the data, calculate KPIs, segment performance, examine monthly trends, query the data with SQL and translate results into testable business questions. The synthetic nature of the data is a deliberate limitation and is stated throughout.
